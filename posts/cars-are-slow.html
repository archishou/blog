Cars are actually pretty slow

November 22, 2026

Cars are slower than I originally thought. I am reading a book called 'Walkable Cities' by Jeff Speck and came across this quote. "The model American puts in 1600 hours to get 7500 miles, less than 5 miles per hour." That was a pretty facinating quote to me. This is a cited text in the book, with the original quote coming from Illich, Toward a History of Needs. I want to read that book next. The argument of the author is that although the speedometer reads 30 miles per hour or whatever when you travel, we aren't accounting for the hidden cost of time it takes in labor to upkeep the car.

As an example, a car could travel 10 miles in an hour. However, if the car cost $30 to buy and it took you two hours of working to get $30. Then really travelling 10 miles took you 1 hour in travel, plus two hours in labor to buy the car. That's 10 miles for 3 hours of time or really 3ish miles per hour. That's a toy example and so I wanted to try looking at real numbers to see how fast cars actually are.

The model we're trying to figure out is speed = miles travelled / (time in car + time for labor). Time for labor is all the time it takes to make money to pay for the car we're actually using to commute. So we've gotta work out what all the costs are for a car. There are many, but the biggest ones are the cost of car, insurance, fuel, maintaince, tolls. Then we've gotta figure out the time costs. There are really just two. One is how long it takes once you're in your car to drive to your destination. The second are other things like filling up for gas, looking for parking, going to the maintance shop and dealing with those people etc.

Monetary Costs: Cost of car, Insurance, Fuel, Maintaince, Tolls.

Time Costs: Time for travel, Filling for Gas, Looking for Parking, Maintenance Shop.

FHWA, The Federal Highway Association, provies a bunch of good data on the average driver. The average driver travelled 13,735 miles over all of 2024.

Computing the average time a person spends in a car per year driving is more complex. TrueDrivingCost has done seem really worth while analysis here that I recommend reading. The general gist of it though is that the FHWA publishes a survey called the NHTS which gets a bunch of partcipants (7500 in 2022), to record for 24 hours every bit of travel they do and a bunch of data about that travel. Then each of those surveyed results are weighted against the census so we can better capture the average american driver. There are two things that matter out of that survey which are how fast are people driving on average and how far are they travelling. We can use both to figure out how long are people driving on average. In the end, we get around 457 hours on average for a driver in a car per year. That works out to 75 minutes per day which seems right for a 30 to 35 minute commute plus some errands driving up the average.

Next is how much does the average person spend on thier car (per year). This one's pretty simple! The Bureu of Labor Statistics publishes basically exactly that. From the 2024 study there's a portion describing vehcile purchasing being 5,337 per year, gas as 2,645 per year and other expenses as 4,206. Thats a total of $12,188 per year on cars. The catch is that this is per household and of course in america, every household owns more than 1 car. BLS publishes that households own on average 1.9 cars. So per car, this is $6414 per year.

For median take home wage, BLS again publishes a bunch of stats. This publishes the median weekly wage which for Q3 in 2026 was $1,239 (seasonally adjusted) or $64,428 per year. This is yearly income before taxes. I assumed single filer status for federal taxes. For an income of $64,428, a single filer will pay $5,551.36 in federal income taxes after the 2026 $16,100 standard deduction, leaving us with $58,876.64. Though there's still state income and payroll taxes to worry about :/. For payroll taxes there's Medicare and Social Security. Again based on the tax codes given by the IRS, the taxes for both of those are $4,928.74 for a new total of $53,947.90 per year. Finally, there are state taxes. State taxes vary a TON so I'm just using a flat 2% as a rough nationwide estimate. That's $1,288.56 from our original income leaving us with $52,659.34 for the year. For 2080 working hours in a year 52,659.34 / 2080 is $25.31 per hour for the average person.

Ok now we have basically all the key numbers. Drivers drive approx 13,735 miles per year. They drive for approx 457 hours per year. They spend $6414 per year on their car. And they make $52,659.34 per year or $25.31 per hour (on average).

First we should figure out how fast the average driver travels today. 13,735 miles per year divided by 457 per year is 30.1 mph. If it costs $6414 per year to operate a car and we on average make $25.31 per hour, then 6414 / 25.31 = 253.4 hours of work per year to afford the car. If our speed was miles travelled / (time driving + time working), then 13,735 / (457 + 253.4) = 19.3mph. So on average, we're all actually driving around 19mph, which is 36% less than what we might expect.

Also, note that if anything this computation is actually underselling it. We're not accounting for any sort of car managment. Time at the gas statation, time spent looking for parking, time looking for where your car is etc etc. I also didn't include local taxes which for places like NYC is .... A LOT. So in reality this number is probably much worse.
